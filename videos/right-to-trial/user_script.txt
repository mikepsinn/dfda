Voice: HeyGen "Nadine" (calm & gentle).

1 Margaret: Margaret is 68 and has Alzheimer's. Researchers have identified 573 existing drugs that might help her. Few have ever been tested for Alzheimer's, so her doctor has no evidence to recommend any of them. And if she takes one anyway, nobody records what happens.

2 Three reasons: There are three reasons. Old drugs can't be patented, so no company pays to test them. Almost no Alzheimer's patients are in any study, so nobody learns from their treatment. And the federal Right to Try law lets drug makers charge only their costs, so they have no reason to take part: only 21 drugs have been made available in over six years.

3 Proof: It doesn't have to be this way. In 2020, Britain built a clinical trial into ordinary hospital care. In under three months, it showed that dexamethasone, a cheap steroid used for decades, cut deaths among the sickest COVID patients by up to a third. It saved about a million lives.

4 The idea: Care-integrated clinical trials would do the same for every disease, in everyday care. Here's Margaret's year.

5 Explore (Step 1): She starts by comparing her options: treatments ranked side by side, each with an outcome label showing who improved, the side effects, the cost and how strong the evidence is.

6 Screened first: An independent board has already screened every option.

7 Doctor, consent and cost (Steps 2-3): Her own doctor recommends one over a video visit, and she signs a plain-language consent: the risks, the unknowns, who pays, and that it's experimental. A charity helps her pay, and no insurer or state program has to.

8 Treatment and safety (Step 4): Her first dose is in week two, at a clinic near home. Memory tests and quick phone check-ins track how she's doing. Any serious side effect reaches the board within days, and it can pause new patients.

9 Results and the loop (Steps 5-6): At six months her outcome is recorded, good, bad or no change, then de-identified and published. Nothing is hidden. Pooled with every other clinic, it updates the label. The next patient starts with better data than Margaret had.

10 Close: Any patient can get treatment through their own doctor. Clinics can afford to offer it. Every result is published, and the next patient learns from Margaret. Learn more at acceleratedmedicine.org.

Sources: 573 drugs — Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2023. 99.8% of Alzheimer's patients in no study — USC Schaeffer Center; Alzheimer's Association, 2026. 21 drugs under federal Right to Try, May 2018 to December 2024 — FDA, via FactCheck.org, 2026. RECOVERY (89 days from the first patient to the dexamethasone result; deaths cut by up to a third in patients on ventilators) — RECOVERY Collaborative Group, New England Journal of Medicine, 2021. About a million lives saved worldwide by March 2021 — NHS England, 2021.
